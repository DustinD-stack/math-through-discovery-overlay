# Unit 1.2 — Benchmark Numbers

- **Unit:** 1.2
- **Domain:** D1 — Number Sense & Composition
- **Purpose:** develop fluent complement pairs to 5 and to 10 (Make 5 folded in per P1
  §3), as relationships rather than isolated memorized facts — the direct prerequisite
  for Unit 1.3's Make 10 strategy.
- **Prerequisites:** Unit 1.1 complete (bond fluency), 0.2.8 (5/10 as landmark quantities).
- **Unlocks:** Unit 1.3 (Make 10 & Flexible Addition to 20).
- **Primary patterns:** Make a Benchmark, Part/Whole.
- **Primary representations:** ten-frame/five-frame (object/picture) → `NumberBond`.
- **Lesson count:** 5 instructional lessons (+ 1 unit mastery check).

---

### 1.2.1 — What Does 5 Need?
- **Type / Difficulty:** Discovery · Entry
- **Teaching goal:** for any number ≤5, name how many more are needed to reach 5.
- **Student question:** "How many more to make 5?"
- **Prerequisites:** 0.2.8, 1.1.4 (missing-part reasoning).
- **New idea:** treating "distance to 5" as a fact worth knowing directly, not
  re-derived by counting every time.
- **Reuses:** missing-part bonds (1.1.4), five-frame (0.2.8).
- **Reasoning pattern(s):** Make a Benchmark.
- **Representation:** Object/Picture (five-frame) → `NumberBond` (whole fixed at 5).
- **Vocabulary:** needs, to make 5.
- **SETUP:** a five-frame with 3 dots filled.
- **SEE:** "How many empty spaces are left?"
- **BREAK:** count or subitize the empty spaces.
- **BUILD:** state the complement: "3 needs 2 more to make 5."
- **TRANSFORM:** the same 3, described as itself or as "2 away from 5" — same value,
  benchmark-relative form.
- **CHECK:** fill the empty spaces and count all 5.
- **Primary example:** 3 needs 2.
- **Second example:** 1 needs 4.
- **Transfer example:** 4 needs 1, without a five-frame shown (mental image only).
- **Misconception:** states the *filled* count again instead of the *missing* count when
  asked "how many more." *Why:* same root cause as 1.1.4's addition-instead-of-difference
  error, now in a benchmark-specific context.
- **Teacher response:** "How many are filled? How many spaces are still empty?"
- **Check strategy:** inverse operation (filled + missing = 5).
- **Mastery evidence:** states the complement to 5 for at least 4 of 5 numbers 1–4,
  without recounting from scratch.
- **Unlocks:** 1.2.2.
- **Visual:** five-frame — no MTD component; `NumberBond` for the symbolic form.
- **Format:** Both. **Presenter:** Optional.

### 1.2.2 — Make 5 With Two Groups
- **Type / Difficulty:** Strategy · Developing
- **Teaching goal:** given two small groups, recognize whether they already make 5, and
  if not, how far off they are.
- **Student question:** "Do these two groups make 5 together? How do you know?"
- **Prerequisites:** 1.2.1.
- **New idea:** applying the complement-to-5 fact to *combining* two given groups, not
  just filling one frame.
- **Reuses:** composing (0.2.3), complements to 5 (1.2.1).
- **Reasoning pattern(s):** Make a Benchmark, Part/Whole.
- **Representation:** Object/Picture → `NumberBond`.
- **Vocabulary:** (reinforce make 5).
- **SETUP:** two small groups shown together (e.g., 2 and 2).
- **SEE:** "Do you think these make exactly 5?"
- **BREAK:** combine and count, or reason from known complements.
- **BUILD:** state whether the total is 5, less than 5, or more than 5, and by how much.
- **TRANSFORM:** the same two groups, seen as a combined total relative to the 5
  benchmark.
- **CHECK:** count the combined group directly to confirm.
- **Primary example:** 2 and 2 (total 4 — one short of 5).
- **Second example:** 3 and 2 (total 5 — exact).
- **Transfer example:** 4 and 3 (total 7 — over 5), extending the reasoning past the
  benchmark itself.
- **Misconception:** assumes any two small groups automatically make 5 because 5 was
  just discussed. *Why:* pattern-matching to the lesson topic instead of actually
  checking — a normal, low-stakes error corrected by the CHECK step itself.
- **Teacher response:** "Let's not guess — how can we be sure?"
- **Check strategy:** recount / inverse operation.
- **Mastery evidence:** correctly classifies 3 pairs of small groups as exactly, under,
  or over 5.
- **Unlocks:** 1.2.3.
- **Visual:** objects/`NumberBond`.
- **Format:** Both. **Presenter:** Optional.

### 1.2.3 — What Does 10 Need?
- **Type / Difficulty:** Discovery · Developing/Secure
- **Teaching goal:** for any number ≤10, name how many more are needed to reach 10 — the
  single most important fact family for Unit 1.3.
- **Student question:** "How many more to make 10?"
- **Prerequisites:** 1.2.1 (same reasoning, new benchmark), 0.2.8 (ten-frame).
- **New idea:** complements to 10 — moving the 1.2.1 idea from 5 to 10.
- **Reuses:** 1.2.1's method, ten-frame (0.2.8).
- **Reasoning pattern(s):** Make a Benchmark.
- **Representation:** Object/Picture (ten-frame) → `NumberBond` (whole fixed at 10).
- **Vocabulary:** (reinforce needs, to make 10).
- **SETUP:** a ten-frame with 7 filled (the exact case Unit 1.3 will use first).
- **SEE:** "How many empty spaces are left?"
- **BREAK:** subitize or count the 3 empty spaces.
- **BUILD:** state "7 needs 3 more to make 10."
- **TRANSFORM:** the same 7, described as itself or as "3 away from 10."
- **CHECK:** fill the empty spaces and count all 10.
- **Primary example:** 7 needs 3.
- **Second example:** 8 needs 2.
- **Transfer example:** 9 needs 1 and 6 needs 4, in the same sitting, building toward
  fluency across the full set.
- **Misconception:** confidently states the complement for 7, 8, 9 (closer to 10, more
  drilled) but struggles for smaller numbers like 3 or 4 (needing 7 or 6). *Why:* the
  frame makes the *small* gap easy to see visually near 10 but the *large* gap harder to
  subitize near the low end — worth deliberately practicing the full range 1–9, not just
  the near-10 cases that Unit 1.3 will use first.
- **Teacher response:** "The gap is bigger this time — can you count the empty spaces, or
  do you already know a fact that helps?"
- **Check strategy:** inverse operation (filled + missing = 10).
- **Mastery evidence:** states the complement to 10 correctly for at least 7 of 9 numbers
  1–9.
- **Unlocks:** 1.2.4, and directly, Unit 1.3.
- **Visual:** ten-frame; `NumberBond`.
- **Format:** Both. **Presenter:** Optional.

### 1.2.4 — Complement Pairs I Know by Heart
- **Type / Difficulty:** Fluency · Secure
- **Teaching goal:** retrieve complements to 10 quickly, without rebuilding a ten-frame
  every time — fluency practice *after* the concept (1.2.3) is secure.
- **Student question:** "Can you answer without the frame this time?"
- **Prerequisites:** 1.2.3 secure.
- **New idea:** none — this is deliberately a Fluency lesson type (§7 of
  `CURRICULUM_ARCHITECTURE.md`): it builds efficient retrieval, it does not introduce a
  new concept.
- **Reuses:** 1.2.3 entirely.
- **Reasoning pattern(s):** Make a Benchmark.
- **Representation:** Symbol only (a bare "___ needs ___" prompt), with the ten-frame
  available as a fallback, not the default.
- **Vocabulary:** (none new).
- **SETUP:** a quick sequence of numbers 1–9 presented one at a time.
- **SEE:** "What does this number need to make 10?"
- **BREAK:** retrieve from memory first; fall back to the ten-frame only if stuck.
- **BUILD:** answer, then move to the next.
- **TRANSFORM:** — (this lesson intentionally does not add a new equivalence; it drills
  the one already established).
- **CHECK:** self-check against a ten-frame for any answer given with hesitation.
- **Primary example:** the full run 1–9, self-paced.
- **Second example:** the same run, repeated, timed informally for personal improvement
  (not a competitive metric).
- **Transfer example:** complements given out of numeric order (e.g., 6, 2, 9, 4) to
  confirm retrieval, not a memorized sequence.
- **Misconception:** recites the complements in a fixed memorized order but cannot answer
  when asked out of order. *Why:* has memorized a sequence, not the individual fact
  relationships — a signal to return to 1.2.3's frame-based reasoning for that specific
  number before drilling further.
- **Teacher response:** "Let's check that one with the frame — does it still work when I
  ask out of order?"
- **Check strategy:** alternate representation (ten-frame, used sparingly as a fallback).
- **Mastery evidence:** answers at least 7 of 9 out-of-order complement prompts within a
  few seconds each, without rebuilding a frame.
- **Unlocks:** 1.2.5; fluency here directly speeds every Unit 1.3 lesson.
- **Visual:** symbol-only prompt; ten-frame as fallback.
- **Format:** Both. **Presenter:** Not needed (a fluency drill does not require a
  presenter on camera).

### 1.2.5 — Which Benchmark Is Closer?
- **Type / Difficulty:** Strategy · Secure/Transfer
- **Teaching goal:** given a number, decide whether 5 or 10 is the more useful nearby
  benchmark to reason from.
- **Student question:** "Is this number closer to 5 or closer to 10?"
- **Prerequisites:** 1.2.1–1.2.4.
- **New idea:** choosing *between* two benchmarks — a light preview of the
  strategy-selection skill fully developed in Unit 1.3.2 ("Choosing to Make Ten").
- **Reuses:** complements to 5 (1.2.1) and to 10 (1.2.3).
- **Reasoning pattern(s):** Make a Benchmark.
- **Representation:** informal number path (0.2.6) with 5 and 10 both marked → `NumberBond`.
- **Vocabulary:** closer to.
- **SETUP:** a number path with 5 and 10 marked; a target number is given (e.g., 6).
- **SEE:** "Is 6 closer to 5 or closer to 10?"
- **BREAK:** find the distance to each benchmark (0.2.7's skill, reused directly).
- **BUILD:** compare the two distances and choose the smaller.
- **TRANSFORM:** the same number (6), reframed relative to whichever benchmark is more
  useful for it.
- **CHECK:** confirm both distances by counting along the path.
- **Primary example:** 6 (closer to 5, by 1, vs. 4 away from 10).
- **Second example:** 8 (closer to 10).
- **Transfer example:** 7 or 8 — genuinely closer to 10 but still meaningfully related to
  5 — an explicit discussion that "closer" doesn't mean "the other one is useless."
- **Misconception:** always defaults to 10 regardless of the actual number, because Unit
  1.3 (which the learner may sense is coming) is "about 10." *Why:* anticipating the
  upcoming strategy rather than reasoning about the specific number — worth surfacing
  explicitly rather than letting it go unexamined.
- **Teacher response:** "Let's actually measure both distances before deciding."
- **Check strategy:** alternate representation (distance-counting on the path confirms
  the choice).
- **Mastery evidence:** correctly identifies the closer benchmark for 4 of 5 numbers
  1–9, with a stated distance-based justification.
- **Unlocks:** the Unit 1.2 Mastery Check; Unit 1.3 (Make 10) directly.
- **Visual:** number path; `NumberBond`.
- **Format:** Both. **Presenter:** Optional.
